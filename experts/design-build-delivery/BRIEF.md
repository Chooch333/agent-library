# design-build-delivery

## Lens

1. Committing a DB price or design fee when the five conditions are missing: well-defined scope, shared understanding of scope, owner construction sophistication, adequate owner staffing, established budget. The budget means the owner's total project budget, already carrying land, design fees, approvals and permits, surveys, FF&E, owner administration, financing, contingency and escalation, and stated as fixed or as moving with the quality chosen; a construction-only figure leaves unknown the share a DB price or GMP has to fit [Songer & Molenaar 1997, abstract; ~ the study surveyed public-sector owners only; NCARB088-03 slides 124, 133, 143; ~ the total-budget reading is inference; EB-073]
2. An owner without the staff or authority to decide on time. DBIA wants the owner "fully engaged and prepared to make the timely decisions necessary", represented by staff with authority to decide [DBIA BP 2023 Executing BP2 f, Procuring BP1 b]
3. Owner criteria loaded with prescriptive requirements or bridging documents. DBIA says these eliminate many of DB's advantages, push selection toward price, and under Spearin shift design risk back to the owner, which breaks single-point responsibility. Where the design-builder accepts them, the line on who carries design risk has to be written into the contract [DBIA position statement "Prescriptive Design and the Use of Bridging Documents" (dbia.org store summary; full copy in a Haines AK public record 2025-12-04); DBIA BP 2023 Procuring BP2 d; ~ the last clause is inference]
4. Assuming owner changes will be rare. On DB, changes from design errors drop but owner-requested changes rise, so the contract needs a fair, fast change process from day one [Perkins 2009, abstract: a small sample from a single governmental owner; DBIA BP 2023 Contracting BP1 e]
5. The designer of record treated as a drawing vendor after award. The subcontract should require the design professionals' regular, active involvement through execution, including construction-phase services [DBIA BP 2023 Contracting BP3 a]
6. Design liability misread from the form. As charted in 2017, AIA A141 reads as strict liability plus a fiduciary duty (Exhibit A §5.6) and lets the DBer rely on owner information (§7.2.5). CD 410 uses "reasonable skill and judgment" and has no reliance statement. Downstream, CD 420 holds the A/E to common-law negligence and B143 to professional skill and care [AGC CD vs AIA chart 2017; ~ that the gap lands on the DBer is inference; the forms may have been revised since 2017]
7. Risk assigned to whoever signs, not to "the party that is best capable of addressing and mitigating the risk" [DBIA BP 2023 Contracting BP1 b]
8. PDB contracted as single-phase DB. Using CD 410 from the start, with a later 410.1 GMP amendment, is "incompatible with a PDB approach". PDB runs a Phase 1 agreement (CD 400), then Phase 2 (CD 410 or 415). CD 411/411.1 were announced to allow several progressive GMPs, with construction starting on earlier phases [ConsensusDocs 2023-03-07; ConsensusDocs 2025-11-19; ~ 411's release (planned Feb 2026) not confirmed]
9. A "progressive" procurement that fixes the price at selection. Under DBIA, the PDB price commitment comes only after scope, schedule and design are agreed. VDOT's I-95/Route 123 PDB capped all phases at award and scored a Phase 1A price [DBIA BP 2023 Procuring BP4 c; VDOT CTB PDB overview 2022-09-20]
10. An undefined off-ramp. DBIA says the procurement must set out the circumstances and notice for ending negotiations, payment for the earned value of DB services, the owner's right to reuse the work product, and its right to take over subcontracts and purchase orders. Failure to agree the construction-phase contract or a GMP is the natural exit. VDOT treats it as termination for convenience. FHWA holds the price negotiations at predetermined design-completion milestones [DBIA BP 2023 Procuring BP4 d; ConsensusDocs 2023-03-07; VDOT CTB 2022; FHWA PDB flyer]
11. Design ownership at an off-ramp read from the wrong form. A141 keeps copyright with the DBer and gives the owner a limited license on termination if it pays and indemnifies. CD 410 vests ownership of the documents, but not copyright, at payment, and lets the owner use them to complete the project on termination if it has paid [AGC CD vs AIA chart 2017; DBIA BP 2023 Procuring BP4 d]
12. Cost built behind closed doors in Phase 1. DBIA calls for "fully transparent pricing" throughout development of scope, schedule and price. Open-book contracts with QBS went with more cohesive teams and lower average cost growth [DBIA BP 2023 Procuring BP4 a; Franz et al. 2017, abstract; VDOT CTB 2022]
13. Builders and trades brought into design late. Delivery methods that involved the builder and specialty trades before schematic design reached higher team integration and controlled schedule growth better. Integration and cohesion stand between the delivery method and the results [Franz et al. 2017, abstract]
14. Pitching DB to owners on old performance numbers. DB's edge over CMR and DBB has narrowed since 1998, except on delivery speed [Franz, Molenaar, Roberts 2020, abstract]
15. Pursuing a procurement that breaks DBIA's own terms: a two-step best-value shortlist of more than three, or a progressive RFQ with no commensurate stipend announced [DBIA BP 2023 Procuring BP3 b, BP4 e]
16. ~ An owner program carried into design that its budget cannot buy. The gap is closed in predesign by cutting size or quality against a low-to-high cost range with caveats and contingency, local builders being a named source for that range; found later in design, it brings redesign, blame and sometimes litigation. On PDB the check sits in Phase 1 validation, before design moves toward a GMP [NCARB088-03 slides 132, 140, 143; ~ the PDB placement is inference; EB-074]
