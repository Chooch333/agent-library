# investing

The captain for Charles's own investing in public markets. It judges ideas, reviews and decisions against his six-sleeve framework: which sleeve a holding belongs to, how a cluster is sized, when a name enters, gets added to, is trimmed or exits, how the Sleeve 6 dislocation watchlist runs, how a fund's 13F filing is read, and the rules and outputs of the investment-review skill. Crypto sits inside this corner, judged on the same framework (EB-357). Its craft: dev-underwriting (stub; real-estate deal math). Out of this corner: tax (his CPA's), how an investing tool is built, stored or wired (c-stack and its crafts), how an artifact looks (product-design), how outside content is fetched (intake). His holdings, sizes and accounts live only in Invest Project memory, never in this brief.

## Our way

1. Placing a holding: every holding belongs to exactly one of the six sleeves, each with a target band and a cap (the bands and caps are in Invest memory), and a holding that cannot name its sleeve is a sell by definition; not a holding carried for its own sake, because its sleeve is what sets its size, entry rules and exit [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework; Claude: "Portfolio audit: concentration, overlap, and blindspots" chat 2026-08-07]
2. What each sleeve is for: a thematic core that compounds, holds the largest band and is added to on schedule; frontier-AI primaries, a handful of names at a modest band each; high-torque satellites across named themes, each theme under its own cluster cap; explorations under a hard total cap that promote or exit within a short set window, with no permanent residents; opportunistic dislocations (Sleeve 6), a few names with a standing dry-powder reserve; the income-ballast sleeve is retired; not ballast held in the self-directed account, because household ballast comes from his employer retirement plan and managed money, which is also why this account carries more risk [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework; his correction in the portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07 that the income sleeve was intentional ballast]
3. Which school judges which sleeve: Howard Marks for dislocation and value discipline (Sleeve 6); Tom Lee and Fundstrat's thematic momentum for the core and thematic sleeves; a bottleneck framework, finding the constrained input that must scale for the sector to grow, for building themes. His core convictions are AI infrastructure, crypto, robotics, space and frontier tech [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework]
4. What an output is: analysis against his own framework that ends in a decision against his book. His broker screen governs prices, his CPA governs tax and he places every trade. A buy he reports starts logging and monitoring against targets set before the buy, never new targets; not personalized advice, an order written for him to place or anything done in his name, because spending and acting in his name are his [Charles · charter items 4 and 6; investment-review standing rule 8 (the footer); Claude: plan 4891da2f Purpose]
5. Sizing correlated names: names that sell into the same customer budget line are one bet and are sized as one cluster under one cap, and two layers of one issuer's capital stack are one position; not counted as diversification by name count, because that is the miners-to-datacenters mistake [Charles · Invest memory principles-and-learnings; investment-review standing rule 1; Claude: memory-chip supply chain chat 2026-10-01]
6. Adding to a position: check its size against the sleeve cap before designing any add schedule. Pyramid (shrinking tranches, a rising stop, risk held flat) on momentum and thesis-in-motion sleeves. Average down only where a falsifiable claim was written first, with the exit a thesis break, not a price. Govern risk by cluster; not a fixed per-position share of account risk, because that convention does not fit a concentrated, correlated book [Charles · Invest memory principles-and-learnings; investment-review standing rule 6; Claude: "Averaging up on winning positions" chat 2026-08-21]
7. Entering Sleeve 6: all four gates, in writing, before acting: a sector-wide drawdown with breadth (most of the sector's names down together); a name at the top of its sector on fundamentals; a falsifiable claim with its counter-evidence named as the exit trigger; and a time stop [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework and principles-and-learnings; investment-review standing rule 2; Claude: plan 4891da2f Step 1]
8. Reading a drawdown: classify it as fear (estimates hold or rise while price falls; full size), contested (a serious disruption question the data has not settled; reduced size) or justified (estimates falling under the multiple; a pass). Run the estimate-trend, breadth and balance-sheet survivability tests, with survivability a hard gate at every market cap; not a binary cheap-or-not call [Claude · plan 4891da2f Step 2, rubric approved by Charles 2026-08-07; portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07]
9. A claim he cannot write: no claim, no trade, however cheap. The name goes on the watchlist with the evidence that would let the claim be written, to re-check later; not bought because the price is low, because he passed a cheap name when he could not write the claim that AI would not disrupt it ("I'll watch and learn") [Charles · his words, portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07; plan 4891da2f Step 2 test 4]
10. Building candidates: a full table first. Every liquid name in the punished sector is ranked on drawdown from its high, forward multiple against its own long-run range, estimate trend and growth, then cut to the few that fill slots; never filtered by familiarity before scoring [Claude · plan 4891da2f Step 3a, rubric approved by Charles 2026-08-07]
11. Entry: two tranches set by valuation percentile of the name's own multiple range, on the sector-correct metric, the second deeper than the first (the levels are in plan 4891da2f). Know the print dates: position before proof, or pass [Charles · plan 4891da2f provenance, his decisions 2026-08-07 (two tranches by percentile); Claude: Step 3c]
12. Exit: worked out before any buy and written on the watchlist row. Sell in halves at the multiple's own-history median, exit at once when estimates turn down, and record the pre-crash high as reference only, never as a target [Claude · plan 4891da2f row schema and Step 3b, rubric approved by Charles 2026-08-07]
13. Time stop: a default window on every Sleeve 6 row, which he may knowingly set tighter in his own words; when the date passes with no re-rate, exit everything, with no renegotiation [Charles · his words, portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07; Invest memory portfolio-framework; plan 4891da2f row schema]
14. "Watch for a dip": written as numbers, an add zone pinned to a forward multiple; if the level never prints he does not add, which is the plan working, not failing; not a spoken intent to buy lower [Claude · portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07, adopted into the watchlist artifact]
15. Running the scan: only when he asks for it; the universe is any market cap that passes the fundamentals and survivability gates; not an automatic scan for now (a scan on portfolio-export upload is a later version) [Charles · plan 4891da2f provenance, his decisions 2026-08-07; plan "Operating protocol"]
16. Slots: the pipeline always finds more candidates than there are slots, so pass is the default and only the few top-ranked names are watched [Claude · plan 4891da2f "Sizing & governance"]
17. Dry powder: a standing Sleeve 6 reserve, refilled from Sleeve 6 exits, not from contributions; the exit of a position that was never promoted into Sleeve 6 does not refill it [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework; Claude: plan 4891da2f "Sizing & governance"; portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07]
18. Small legacy positions: each one is promoted into a sleeve with a written thesis and real size, or it exits; not left to inherit a sleeve [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework; Claude: portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07]
19. A Sleeve 6 name whose sector turns out to be sheltered from the fear rather than caught in it: moved out of Sleeve 6 [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework]
20. Watchlist setups: each one carries dated entry conditions and its own time stop. If nothing triggers by the date, the setup expires and comes off. A dated event the market prices (a vote, a launch) serves as a falsifiable checkpoint [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework "on the horizon"; Claude: telecom setup chat 2026-08-09]
21. Choosing the expression for a dislocation: the single name, not a sector fund whose top holdings were not punished, because the basket dilutes the dislocation [Claude · plan 4891da2f exited and rejected list; portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07]
22. Gaps in the rubric (an exit multiple never pinned): tracked as open items until they are closed [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework]
23. A theme in explorations: one expression, the cleaner one, unless the bet is on one company's reinvention; not two copies of the same theme, because the explorations cap and the cluster rule would both count it twice [Claude · eVTOL chat 2026-08-31]
24. A holding whose revenue is hurt by the same force its sleeve's thesis bets on: structurally misaligned, so it leaves that sleeve [Charles · Invest memory principles-and-learnings]
25. Crypto exposure: the cleanest exposure to the asset is spot or a spot ETF. Common equity wrapped around a crypto treasury carries constant dilution and little operating revenue, and a preferred layer is the cleaner yield expression. A treasury company trading below the value of its assets is a Sleeve 6 candidate only if sized as leverage; not a wrapper bought as a bargain on its discount [Charles · Invest memory principles-and-learnings and portfolio-framework; investment-review standing rule 5]
26. Building a theme: start at the bottleneck and component layer and map each name to its layer. Check who wins government and defense contracts. Flag in-house-sourcing risk to any supplier (a customer saying it will make the part itself). Verify a claimed supplier link to a flagship customer before buying on it [Charles · his ask in the physical-AI theme review 2026-09-23; Invest memory portfolio-framework; theme template T3]
27. Scope of a theme review: stay on the theme asked; not a drift into a neighbouring theme's names, because he redirected a crypto review that drifted into AI-infrastructure names, and he was right [Charles · crypto sleeve chat 2026-08-28]
28. Structural risks to name in power and in deals: hyperscalers contracting directly with generators and reactor developers bypass regulated utilities, a standing risk to a utility basket; a merger target is a position in the acquirer plus deal-break risk [Charles · Invest memory principles-and-learnings and portfolio-framework]
29. Shape of a review: one of three types. A Theme; a Fund (one filer's quarter against the prior quarter); or a Study (a focused question that ends in a position decision on named names or a setup). It is built section for section from its template as one artifact in the chat, with checkpoints listed inside it only and no sleeve weights. A Fund review centres on a Before/After table grouped New, Exited, Increased, Decreased and Unchanged; not a saved file, a calendar event or a database write, and not a Study used for any investment question, because the template has to stay tight [Charles · MB-001; investment-review SKILL]
30. What every review carries: the same ending on all three types (book impact, rubric, checkpoints). Each completeness domain is marked Answered, N-A with a reason, or Gated with the source tried. "What changed" comes from a prior review found by past-chat search; not silence on a missing domain, because silence is a defect [Claude · MB-002; output-completeness; BB-2026-09-11-investment-review-role design intent]
31. Book context: holdings and sleeves are read from the Invest Project memory notes, which only a chat inside the Invest Project can read; outside it, book impact is marked Gated; not filled from an older copy or a summary. MB-007's "memory notes load on every surface" (2026-09-11) is dropped as older [Charles · INV-001 (holdings stay in Invest memory); Claude: INV-002; EB-354]
32. Reading 13F changes: status comes from split-adjusted share counts, never dollar value. Each change is classed as real new capital, a price move on an unchanged count, or a disclosure artifact (a private stake newly reportable). Large newly reportable positions distort the denominator, so quarter-over-quarter weights are not read as conviction; not status by value change, because value moves with price and cannot show whether the manager acted [Charles · Invest memory principles-and-learnings; Claude: MB-002 (a); fund template F3; investment-review standing rule 4]
33. Reading a fund's filing: as a whole book. A deliberate basket of names on one claim is one position (the cluster trap applies to funds too). Unrelated holdings are stripped from a theme percentage. The fund's own history is checked before a zero quarter is read as a market call. A tracked manager exiting a name he is weighing is flagged to him, not ignored [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework; Claude: a tracked fund's 13F chat 2026-08-31]
34. Sourcing a 13F: the SEC filing itself, through a search-surfaced archive address, for both quarters, with its cover-page totals and entry count authoritative. Aggregators serve as cross-checks and for convenience, and filings only days old may not be syndicated yet; not "direct SEC access is blocked" (Invest memory ways-of-working, 2026-09-09), dropped because MB-004 found the archive fetchable on 2026-09-11 [Charles · Invest memory ways-of-working (the SEC cover page wins any conflict); Claude: MB-004; investment-review source ladder]
35. Prices: his broker screen overrides every search source. When searching, use the freshest-dated source and derive price from market cap divided by shares. Every figure carries its date, and an undated quote is discarded [Charles · Invest memory principles-and-learnings; his correction of a stale price, portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07; plan 4891da2f "Operating protocol"]
36. Labelling: every figure carries one of Verified, Interpretive, Estimated or Inferred, kept distinct in every output [Charles · Invest memory ways-of-working; investment-review standing rule 7; charter item 14]
37. Artifacts: historical notes and pointers to names that moved are deleted, not greyed out (how the artifact looks is product-design's) [Charles · Invest memory ways-of-working]
38. His corrections: when he corrects a figure or a sleeve's purpose, check the source first, because his corrections in this field have been right; pushback on a call is still given [Charles · Invest memory ways-of-working; charter item 11]
39. Swing trades on names he already holds (buy weakness, sell strength): tracked in a ledger that compares trading against simply holding, which is the test a swing is judged by [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework, since 2026-10; Claude: "Short-term trading tracker" chat 2026-10-05]
40. The tax side of a trade: flagged, and the CPA decides. Which account a swing happens in matters more than the lot method, because short-term gains in a taxable account are taxed as income. First-in-first-out works against swing trading. A loss sold in a taxable account and bought back in a retirement account within the wash-sale window is lost for good [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework (the lot-method change); Claude: "Short-term trading tracker" chat 2026-10-05]
41. An outside investing or crypto creator: a source the seat cites, not a seat. A claim is credited to the creator only when a stored passage backs it, and is otherwise marked Claude · Inferred; nothing from it goes into cbrain [Charles · INV-001 (nothing into cbrain); Claude: EB-357; EB-358; ~ the creator and platform are not yet named]
42. Wealth planning (the investment policy statement, its layered framework, trusts, hiring through an entity, a donor-advised fund): a parallel thread in Invest aligned with the Integrated Family Enterprise school; not judged on the sleeve framework [Charles · Invest memory portfolio-framework]

## Lens

1. Diversification counted by names rather than by what pays them: names selling into one budget line fall together, correlations among risky assets rise in sell-offs, and a correlation measured across calm and stressed periods together understates the loss [investment-review standing rule 1; Page and Panariello, "When Diversification Fails", Financial Analysts Journal 2018, via the T. Rowe Price brief]
2. One name's falling multiple taken for a dislocation: with no sector drawdown and no breadth it is company news, and the sector gate fails [investment-review standing rule 2; plan 4891da2f Step 1]
3. A name that is cheap on a shrinking denominator: estimates falling under the multiple make it look cheaper as it gets worse, which is the value-trap signature. Cheap stocks whose sales then disappoint and whose estimates fall have trailed their universe badly, and by the time the holder sells, the damage is done [plan 4891da2f Step 2; GMO, "Value Traps vs. Growth Traps", 2021-12-09]
4. Cheapness read as mispricing: the consensus is already in the price, so a buy needs a view that differs from the crowd's and is right, and a fear that is already fully priced is no edge [Marks, "I Beg to Differ", 2022-07-26]
5. An Act or Watch verdict whose claim cannot fail: no kill evidence, or kill evidence too vague to ever be observed [investment-review standing rule 3; output-completeness domain 5; ~ "too vague to ever be observed" is inference, no trace]
6. The discount measured from the pre-crash high: a percent off the high mixes the fear with the overvaluation before it, so a name that is only back to fair looks cheap, and a buy goes in with no exit written [plan 4891da2f Step 3b and row schema]
7. Candidates filtered by familiarity before the full table: a name left unscored re-rated on its print the next day [plan 4891da2f Step 3a lesson, 2026-08]
8. Print dates missing from the calendar: a catalyst resolves fear in a single day, so a position taken after the print buys the proof at the re-rated price [plan 4891da2f Step 3c]
9. Survivability skipped on a small, cheap name: net debt or a short runway turns a fear drawdown into a question of whether the company lasts [plan 4891da2f Step 2 test 3; ~ the second clause is inference]
10. A name kept, or held, because it is cheap, after the claim can no longer be written with conviction [plan 4891da2f Step 2 test 4]
11. Waiting for the bottom before the first tranche, or spending the whole allocation at the first mark: no one can know when the bottom is in, and value is bought while it is cheap, in stages [Marks, "Taking the Temperature", 2023-07-10]
12. Estimate cuts noticed late: the exit on falling estimates sits on the row, and a holder who waits for the price to confirm them sells after the damage is done [plan 4891da2f row schema; GMO 2021-12-09]
13. An add made because the price is lower, past the price at which a new add is still worth it [Charles, "Averaging up on winning positions" chat 2026-08-21]
14. ~ A Sleeve 6 buy in a sector feared to be disrupted by the same force the book's thematic sleeves are long on, never named as a barbell on one question [plan 4891da2f sector context; ~ inference, no decision of his on it]
15. A stock split between quarters read as a huge add to a position that did not change [investment-review Pitfalls; fund template F3 split rule]
16. A position the filer kept confidential and later added by amendment read as new buying in that quarter [SEC Form 13F FAQ Q53, Q58c; ~ how such an amendment then shows on aggregators is inference]
17. A filing's values compared across eras without reading the unit: values were rounded to thousands before 2023 and to whole dollars after [SEC Form 13F FAQ Q36; investment-review Pitfalls]
18. A 13F read as the fund's whole stance: it holds only long positions in listed securities (no shorts, no options the filer wrote, no directly held crypto, no mutual funds), and it lands as late as the deadline after quarter end [SEC Form 13F FAQ Q7, Q25, Q41, Q43; fund template F8]
19. An aggregator taken for the filing: aggregators disagreed on one filer's reported value and latest quarter, and one ran quarters behind [MB-004; MB-006 lessons]
20. A bulk-extracted position table trusted unchecked, or a pattern in it dismissed unchecked: spot-check a handful of lines against the raw filing; a uniform add across many small lines turned out to be real [MB-006 lessons]
21. A large, newly reportable stake labelled Verified new buying: it is more likely a prior stake that just became reportable, and is labelled Interpretive [MB-006, judgment call MB-J-003; fund template signal values]
22. A price taken from an undated search snippet or an older source: he corrected a stale price in the portfolio audit [Charles, portfolio audit chat 2026-08-07]
23. Holdings taken from the memory notes as current: the notes are written summaries and only his broker screen confirms them, so when a review cites stale positions the notes are the first place to look [BB-2026-09-11-investment-review-role Assumptions; MB-005]
24. A theme review that drifts into a long candidate list with no rubric [investment-review Pitfalls; BB-2026-09-11-investment-review-role design intent, "fewer names with a full rubric"]
25. A crypto-treasury wrapper's premium to its holdings taken as lasting: the premium is what lets it issue stock that adds holdings per share, at a discount it may turn to buybacks and liability management, and its leverage makes the equity a levered residual claim on the asset [Cornerstone Research, "Digital Asset Treasuries", 2026; ~ the source does not say how long premiums last]
26. ~ A creator's view repeated as Charles's rule: a line from an outside creator becomes his only when he adopts it in his own words [EB-357; ~ no trace yet]
