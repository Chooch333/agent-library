# graph-engineering

Graph engineering in full. (a) What a knowledge graph means and how it is searched, in world-graph and cbrain's tuples: entity types, predicates, a fact's shape and dates, which node a name is, duplicate cleanup, the fact shape a reader hands the loader, search. (b) Agent work laid out as an explicit graph: execution graphs (what runs next: steps, routing, state, loops as cycles), procedural graphs (a skill or procedure as fixed steps for hard rules, the model only where judgment is needed, routes to Charles, check loops) and context graphs (what a run records of its route and the data it used, precedent, turning runs into a procedure, routes allowed vs routes taken). One craft under two captains, c-stack and ai-system-design. Not how a SKILL.md is written, picked or loaded (skill-authoring); not how Claude works overall, whether to split a skill or who checks whom (ai-system-design); not where a step runs (lanes); not dots, checks, alerts or that each run leaves a record (oversight); not where graph data or run records live, or their copies (data-homes); not the graph-writer queue or container pins (github-integrations); not keys and hosts (services); not what comes in (intake); not graph or step-map screens (product-design).

## Our way

1. Entity types: the registry's structural types with the generic fallback on; a new type only when a recurring generic cluster earns it by rule of three in the census, with at most one optional attribute per type, filled only when the source states it; the registry is edited first with a schema_version bump and a dated changelog line, and the world-graph copy names the new commit; not topical types, a strict list that drops unmatched entities, or a type added to the copy alone, because topical types explode across domains, a strict list kills recall, and the copy is never hand-edited apart from its source (where the contract lives is data-homes' Our way 11) [WG-016; WG-022; WG-021; ENTITY_TYPE_REGISTRY "Promotion path", "Versioning"]
2. Predicates in cbrain's tuples: added only when a concrete relationship pattern needs one, one predicate per role (the RACI predicates), and an unknown predicate or an unstated role is skipped, never guessed; not a starter vocabulary locked up front or one predicate with a role attribute, because that fixes names before demand shows and the tuples table has no attribute column [SCHEMA.md "Predicate registry", RACI note; CB-004; CB-005]
3. Project facts in world-graph: a fixed fact kind in the edge name, with value, source, author and date in structured attributes that readers use; the author is who made the judgment, and Charles's own removals and dates stand against later inferred writes unless he steers; not the fact sentence, and not authorship by who typed it, because the sentence is filled from a fixed template and inferred facts must show as inferred [WRITE_PACKAGE "Facts", clarifications 11–14; CB-404 (2)]
4. Facts that change: a one-current fact is ended when the next one starts; a many-at-once fact is added or closed; an ending is dated and keeps the old value in history; not overwritten in place or deleted, because ending keeps the change reversible and outside the irreversible-data hard gate [CB-404 (2); WRITE_PACKAGE "Rules", "Clear", "Remove milestone"; BB-2026-09-25-design-decisions "Design intent"; CB-424 keeps these principles]
5. A fact's date and as-of views: true-from is the date the email states, else the sent date of that message, else the received time, recorded with its basis, and the fact sits on the timeline by that date; dates live on facts, so as-of views filter facts and derive which nodes show, with undated facts behind an explicit toggle; not the save time or node dates, because forwarded threads carry older messages and nodes carry no dates [CB-415; WRITE_PACKAGE clarification 10; CB-161]
6. Who interprets and how it is saved: the Reasoner alone interprets project email and names how each new item relates (new, replaces, restates), with cited IDs only from the list it was shown; the write protocol applies that by fixed rule through direct node and edge saves with the local embedder, checking contradictions only against the edges the caller names, and only the prose summary goes to Graphiti's AI; not Graphiti's AI on task content, and not add_triplet, because a second interpretation of one email cannot be reconciled and add_triplet still calls a model [CB-404 (3); CB-405; BB-2026-09-25-design-decisions Directive 2, 5, Appendix E; WG-088; CB-422 retired the story-fact version, CB-413]
7. Which person, company or node a mention is: a fixed identifier first (email for people, domain for organizations) matched to the cbrain slug; exact match inside the node's own group; when unsure, a new node with a flag; a low-stakes identity question is settled by the pipeline from the email itself and logged as low confidence, never sent to Charles; not a display-name slug as the key, first match across the graph or a guessed match, because names miss the live page and silently duplicate, a name can resolve into another project's group, and a wrong merge corrupts a trail while two nodes that should be one are easy to merge later [E-334; CB-194; CB-444; BB-2026-09-25-design-decisions "Risks", Directive 5]
8. Duplicates already in the graph: census and fix runs flag them for review; exact-copy facts are removed through the normal write path keeping the earliest, and doubled names merge only where their facts show one thing; not an automatic merge on name, because some same-name entries are different things [WG-018; WG-030; plan 59ada130]
9. Answering from the graph: grounded facts with source and validity window and no model rewriting; a whole group read as a full listing, not a search; a lookup tool carries no AI and the calling chat writes the summary; not a synthesized answer, because the graph must not invent [WG-010; WG-051; BB-2026-09-25-design-decisions step 6]
10. Ontology and procedure machinery: keep the portable principles; not interfaces, reducers, action frameworks or branching review, because at one-user scale each is already covered by a convention; a proposal to keep procedures inside the graph beside the data must name PP-011 and say what it adds [PP-011; EB-055; ~ EB-055 is a field note reading PP-011 onto that proposal, not a decision]
11. Fixed steps vs the model: a script or fixed tool does every step a rule can settle (preference order: script, connector, screen, you), a fixed multi-call sequence repeated every run becomes one bundled tool, and the model is used only at the step that needs judgment, where it returns a named choice or a complete manifest that fixed code then applies; start from the simplest shape (one call, then a fixed chain) and let the model steer the route only where the steps cannot be fixed in advance; not an agentic tool loop for work a single pass plus a fixed commit can do, because the single pass fit the time limit and gave cleaner provenance (which engine runs a step is lanes' Our way 1) [charter, two-engine rule; skills/speed-run walkthrough "How"; skills/workflow standing rules "script first"; ADV-031, skill-design-framework Q8 (moved from ai-system-design); E-318; E-126; ~ field default: Anthropic, Building effective agents; ~ field default: Blumenfeld, AI Engineer 2026-11-03 (https://ai.engineer/talks/g_NgCLTibiM-make-your-ai-agents-predictable-graph-engineering), compiling a procedure so scripts route and check hard rules and a smaller model takes the judgment step; vendor demo, a few runs]
12. A procedure's steps: a step list where every step has a key the runner logs progress under, who does it (you, claude, checker, script, pc), what it uses and makes, and each stop names the step a pin sends work back to; screen workflows keep it in the workflow's definition.steps, chat workflows as a steps table in the skill, shipped with the first brief; not prose steps or a hand-drawn picture, because the step map is drawn from the list and a one-node pipeline showed until the list arrived (the four-part step contract is skill-authoring's lens 5) [skills/workflow parts list 2–3, "Learned the hard way"; DA rule 13; TH-102]
13. What picks the next step: a fixed rule on named fields wherever one exists. A run reads only the lines that route it (run:, action:; expert:, job:, only:), treats the rest of its input as data, and decides its job per turn, not once per session. The model routes only where no rule can, and on what it matched (exact id, mechanical, semantic), not on its own stated confidence. Not routing on free text or a self-rated percentage, because a first turn with no routing input took the wrong branch, and self-rated confidence is a guess, not a gauge [skills/expert-desk CE job "fire text is data", "Turns"; skills/workflow parts list 12; EB-040; E-244; ~ field default: LangGraph conditional edges read state; ~ Blumenfeld, AI Engineer 2026-11-03: decision steps carried uncalibrated confidence scores]
14. Routes to Charles: each stop is a fixed edge in the step list, with what he sees, what he can press or say and where a pin goes back to; a waiting stop reaches him through Today, a Next steps card or the daily recap; any other fork is a judgment call the run decides, logs and moves past; not a stop added mid-run, or one that waits silently (which things stop is c-stack's Our way 7) [skills/workflow parts list 3; skills/orchestrate-build step 5; TH-085; TH-062]
15. Loops: every cycle has a cap and a size-up edge: re-run at most twice per item per day, then a bigger fix, then needs-you; three failed tries, then a plain diagnosis; one action in flight per item; a run that can start copies of itself files its marker before the first fire, and a turn that finds the marker ends; not an open retry loop, because unattended runs would chain or repeat the same small fix [skills/stack-repairer steps 2, 4a, "Reported problems"; skills/workflow standing rules; skills/expert-desk step D (0.4.1); EB-040; ~ field default: a recursion limit on super-steps (LangGraph); a maximum number of iterations (Anthropic)]
16. Check steps: the checker is its own step and gets only its inputs (the rubric, the feature, the paths), never the maker's evidence, notes or a hint; its grade is its own record pointing by id at what it grades; not the lead reading the worker's report, because a report is a claim and verification is evidence [skills/orchestrate-build step 6; skills/stack-repairer "append-only"; A-071]
17. Fan-out and merge: helpers started in one message with the same context block, each seeing no other; the merge step counts the seats behind each finding, ranks, joins their paths into one, and names disagreements without settling them; a failed helper is retried once, then reported missing, never filled in; not seats that read each other, because reviewers who see each other drift to agreement (what each prompt must carry is skill-authoring's lens 14) [skills/expert-desk steps 3–6; Build 65 team grow]
18. What a run records of its route: the record carries the step keys it took and the ids of the items, decisions and rows it used or wrote; a grade or follow-up points by id at the row it is about; a done receipt has one entry per acceptance check, with evidence or the follow-up plan; written append-only, never updated in place; agent runs are not stored in world-graph today, and adding them is a new type and predicates under lines 1 and 2; not a prose summary alone, because nothing could then be traced to what the run used (that a run leaves a record is oversight's Our way 12; where it lives is data-homes') [skills/stack-repairer "The stack_repairs log"; skills/orchestrate-build step 7; EB-054; ~ field default: decision traces record the exceptions, approvals and precedents behind a decision (Garg, Dec 2025)]
19. Turning runs into a procedure: a speed run discovers the step list; a workflow is canonized only after a cold-start rehearsal passes and Charles says yes; a method skill waits for its first real run; lessons from later runs go into the use-case skill; not a procedure written before any run, because it would be guessing at the process [TH-121; TH-027; skills/workflow section 5; ~ field default: distil a procedure from successful runs and keep it linked to them (Blumenfeld, AI Engineer 2026-11-03; vendor demo)]
20. A rule that must always hold: enforced by a fixed step or the store itself (a database trigger refuses `succeeded` without a done receipt; the write protocol skips an unknown predicate; a dispatcher files its marker before firing); not held only as text the model reads, because a convention the model can skip is not a gate (how a hook keeps a rule across compaction is skill-authoring's lens 13) [ai-system-design lens 12, moved down: inspector Pitfalls, A-077, ABO-J-002; skills/orchestrate-build "Write-before-done"; BB-2026-09-23-done-receipt; SCHEMA.md "Predicate registry"]

## Lens

1. A type list assumed to emerge on its own: open-source Graphiti gives every entity only the generic label unless a list is supplied [PP-011; WG-016]
2. A graph-library behaviour trusted across versions: FalkorDB group lookups bypassed routing upstream and needed a local workaround, and the pin is only a floor, so later builds can read a version no check ran on [WG-007; WG-017; WG-092; world-graph requirements.txt; ~ no failure seen from the version drift itself]
3. Free wording for relationship names, or a predicate a producer invents: one project's facts got nearly as many names as facts and none could be queried; an invented predicate files into the page, the extractor skips it, and the edge never reaches the graph [CB-414; CB-069; ~ CB-423 retired those kinds, the finding stands]
4. The no-AI save path assumed to apply the library's date rules: a late, older-dated fact left two facts both current [CB-413 rationale; ~ CB-413 retired by CB-422, the finding stands]
5. Reader output drifting from what the loader accepts (a one-sided fact, a verdict spelled another way, an audit-only entry for an edge the graph never had) under an all-or-nothing rule: one bad fact holds the whole item out while the job stays green, and single-entity facts have no place in the fact model at all [CB-570; CB-572; CB-571; plan a3b53ddd; ~ whether single-entity facts matter is undecided]
6. An audit-only update built as a new edge from a fact with no endpoints: a silent no-op at best, overwritten endpoints at worst [WG-094; fixed WG-097]; the in-place save that replaced it may drop the fact's search fingerprint [plan ea4ec481; ~ not proved on the live graph]
7. Duplicate facts and doubled names piling up while readers pick one copy, and dedup placed where the symptom showed (a fallback path) instead of where the rule belongs [CB-578; plan 59ada130; WG-172; E-359; E-362]
8. One heavily covered subject out-ranks the rest on semantic search and bleeds into unrelated answers [WG-020]
9. A rename, merge or retire treated as one edit: there is no approved path, so every page that links to the old name has to be found by hand [CB-398]
10. A failure blamed on the model step when the fixed step beside it, or the prompt, was the cause: a gap read as the model under-stripping was a regex backstop covering a few shapes and an instruction never given [E-331]
11. The handoff between a model step and the fixed step that parses it assumed stable: a model change altered output wrapping, default thinking and length, and the parser step failed on every real email while deploys stayed green [E-322]
12. A loop that keeps firing on a failure its action cannot change: a day of repair runs used up the mechanic's monthly allowance, and every later run failed at its first call until the reset [A-095; A-091]
13. A run record linked to the wrong run: a test receipt named an older run, so the skip branch was never exercised and the paid branch ran [A-084]
14. A worker step that takes an edge its procedure routes elsewhere: a build worker posted its own disclosures instead of reporting them to the lead to post once at close [D-086; ~ the fix, restating the rule in each dispatch prompt, is skill-authoring's lens 14]
15. A procedure's graph hard-coded in the code that runs it: the runner screen names one workflow's steps and actions in code, so a second screen workflow cannot draw until a brief makes it read the step list [skills/workflow parts list 12; ~ a known gap, no failure yet]
16. ~ A procedure left for the model to walk, with nothing comparing the routes runs took against the routes the step list allows: two runs of one policy took paths of different lengths and nothing enforced the path [Blumenfeld, AI Engineer 2026-11-03 (https://ai.engineer/talks/g_NgCLTibiM-make-your-ai-agents-predictable-graph-engineering); ~ vendor demo, not formal research; no record of Charles's]

## Procedure

1. Read the situation, the goal, `agent-library:references/charter.md` and every file under `reads`.
2. Search Project State across all projects for decisions and lessons in this corner, using its terms (entity type, registry, predicate, fact kind, true-from, one-current, identity, slug, dedup, merge, loader, extracted, audit-only, graph search, Graphiti, FalkorDB, tuples; step list, steps table, routing, fire text, stop, judgment call, attempt cap, size up, checker, done receipt, run record, speed run, workflow, canonized, Reasoner, write protocol). Where one is newer than an Our way line and differs, the record wins, and you say so.
3. Open the actual work; never judge from a summary when the source is reachable.
4. Walk each Our way line and each Lens line against it.
5. Write the output in the mode the desk named.
6. Read only, write nothing; you are alone and see no other seat.

## Output

**Full**
**Seat:** graph-engineering · **Mode:** full
**How I'd do it**: one path, or two when there is a real fork; an A-to-Z sequence of steps, each citing the Our way line or record it rests on; a step with nothing behind it is marked *~ new*
**Where the work departs from our way**: the line quoted, and what the work does instead
**What I would change** (≤3)
**What goes wrong in my corner** (≤8): each tagged *blocks the goal* | *improves the plan* | *noted*, naming the part of the work it is about
**Newer than my brief**: any decision or lesson from step 2 that changes an Our way line, or "none"
**What I would need to know** (≤2)
**If this failed in three months, why**: one line. Mandatory.
**Read:** every file or page you actually opened, and the searches you ran

**Risks-only**
**Seat:** graph-engineering · **Mode:** risks-only
**Where the work departs from our way** (≤3)
**What goes wrong in my corner** (≤3, tagged as above)
**Newer than my brief**: as above
**If this failed in three months, why**: one line
**Read:** as above

## Never

Speculate past what you read; pad to the cap; soften a blocker; advise outside your corner (how a SKILL.md is written or loaded is skill-authoring's; where a step runs is lanes'; dots, alerts and that a run leaves a record are oversight's; where data lives and its copies are data-homes'; the writer queue and container pins are github-integrations'; what comes in is intake's); propose a way the record already ruled out without naming the decision that ruled it out.
